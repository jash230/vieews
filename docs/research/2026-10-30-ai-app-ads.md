# AI-generated ads that worked: research for the /make-ad skill
*Generated: 2026-10-30 | Sources: 24 | Confidence: medium (the process write-ups are first-hand; most performance numbers are vendor claims)*

## Executive summary

The best-known AI ads (Kalshi's NBA Finals spot, PJ Ace's fake pharma ad, CEAT) were made with the same pipeline: a human writes a funny or strange concept, an LLM turns each shot into a long, self-contained video prompt, a video model generates many takes, and a human keeps the best 5–25%. There is no secret prompt. The concept carries the ad, and the prompts are detailed shot descriptions.

The app ads that actually drove installs (Cal AI, Umax, RizzGPT) were not cinematic. They were creator-style videos and photo slideshows. They showed the app's one "magic moment" in the first 5–10 seconds, tested 5–10 hooks at a time, and posted several times a day.

For an unattended pipeline, the hard parts are choosing the best take with no human watching, the cost of AI video, and each platform's limits.

## 1. The famous AI ads and how they were made

**Kalshi, NBA Finals (June 2025).** Made by PJ Accetturo with Veo 3 in two days for about $2,000. It aired during Game 3 on YouTube TV.
- Process: rough script → Gemini/ChatGPT turns each shot into a Veo prompt → generate → edit in CapCut/Premiere ([Informediation repost of his X thread](https://www.informediation.com/blog/2025/07/02/kalshi-tv-ad-made-with-veo-3/), [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/ai-prompts-used-to-make-the-nba-finals-kalshi-ad-2025-6), [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/ai-ad-kalshi-nba-finals/)).
- Concept rule: "crazy people doing crazy things while showcasing your brand", with unhinged street-interview energy.
- Prompting rules he published:
  - Ask for prompts 3–5 at a time, because quality drops with more.
  - Each prompt must describe the scene as if the model has never seen the shot before or after it. Re-describe the setting, the character and the tone every time to keep them consistent.
  - Don't let the LLM describe background music, or it gets mixed too loud.
  - Repeat any accent you want.
- Yield: **300–400 generations for 15 usable clips**, so roughly 95% thrown away.
- His conclusion: "the most valuable skill… is comedy writing".

**PJ Ace's fake pharma ad.** About $500 of Veo credits in 6–12 hours, with about 30 clips generated to get 10 usable ones. The script came from LLM brainstorming in an SNL style, with the best lines fed back in to get more like them. Each dialogue line became one shot, and each prompt covered action, pose, shot type, camera and dialogue ([write-up](https://villager1598.substack.com/p/how-a-500-ai-video-outperformed-a)).

**PJ Ace, later process (Oct 2025).** Script → shot list → **generate a still image for each shot first** → animate that still with Veo (frames-to-video), reusing the image prompt plus a camera move and dialogue. Starting from stills is cheaper and keeps characters consistent. He also recommends "familiar with the foreign": recognizable IP or history mixed with something unexpected, plus comedic contrast ([podcast notes](https://readray.ai/en/notes/sora-2-20251106/ai-videos-million-views-veo-sora-demo)).

**CEAT tyres (Sep 2025).** ChatGPT for copy, scenes and prompts → Sora for keyframes → Veo 3 to animate each keyframe → captions and sound design. The warning: if you need the same character across scenes, generate a frame for every scene first ([Parul Dhand](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/parul-dhand_here-is-a-simple-breakdown-of-our-most-viral-activity-7372137540440707072-xdss)).

## 2. What drove installs for apps

**Cal AI / Umax / RizzGPT (Blake Anderson and team).**
- **Sell the desire, not the feature.** The winners tapped core desires: attraction, social status, self-improvement and entertainment. "Don't tell me what your app does, tell me how it will make my life better" ([transcript](https://pickscribe.com/v/jrNIZqL22Ag)).
- **Test hooks, then double down.** Umax tested 5–10 hooks at once, for example "POV: you use the Umax app to glow up" (before/after rating 55 → 95) and "best haircuts for your face shape". They then scaled the winners ([same](https://pickscribe.com/v/jrNIZqL22Ag)). Two $50 micro-creator posts got RizzGPT 5–10M views and 200K downloads in a week ([Better Launch](https://www.betterlaunch.co/playbooks/episodes/blake-3apps)).
- **Show the magic moment early.** Cal AI asks creators for only two things: show the three core screens (camera → analyzing → result) and show them in the first 5–10 seconds, before viewers drop off. Their result screens are designed to be readable from a distance and carry the logo ([Subversive podcast](https://www.usetranscribe.io/yt/gB0C7NA5fXo/cal-ai-growth-tiktok)).
- **Six-beat structure** of a Cal AI reel: hook (0–5s) → everyday routine → app revealed (~15s) → micro-demo in three verbs ("shoot, save, done") → back to life → comments asking "what app is that?". There's no formal call to action ([Jesse Qin](https://jesse-qin.medium.com/gen-z-reel-to-habit-cal-ais-viral-blueprint-a2cc44c22651)).
- **Stealth-promo format:** copy a format that's already going viral, then drop the app in naturally mid-video ([Sebastian Stef](https://sebastianstef.com/resources/cal-ai-case-study)).
- **Slideshows:** their breakout TikTok format was photo slideshows with bait text. They cost nothing to make and kept going viral ([same](https://sebastianstef.com/resources/cal-ai-case-study)).
- **Volume:** posts several times a day, nearly every day, across multiple accounts. "They're not chasing viral hits" ([same](https://sebastianstef.com/resources/cal-ai-case-study)).
- **Organic vs paid:** organic winners differ from paid-ad winners, so paid ads needed their own content ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/cal-ai-s-viral-playbook-from-one-influencer-story-post-to-a-10m-second-act)).

**AI UGC (AI actors talking to camera).** Agencies run 3–5 angles × 5–8 hooks each, make them with AI actors (HeyGen, Arcads, Captions), keep the winners and brief human creators on those. Tutorial and app-review formats are claimed to get 45% more installs per impression than testimonials ([Mobihunter](https://www.mobihunter.co/blog/ai-ugc-for-mobile-app-ads), agency data). Creatify claims its AI ad cost 28% less per result than the best influencer ad ([Creatify](https://creatify.ai/case-study/ai-video-ads-vs-traditional-video-ads), vendor data). A three-month user of an automated slideshow tool said the best posts weren't pure AI: they paired automated assembly with real assets and creative direction ([AIPipPip](https://aipippip.net/post/reelfarm-review)).

**UGC prompt anatomy.** Typical elements: "Vertical 9:16 selfie video, [creator] in [ordinary place], eye-level phone camera, slight hand shake, natural light, [emotion], says: '…'". Handheld shake reads as user-made rather than studio-made ([Luma](https://lumalabs.ai/news/ugc-style-ai-video-prompts)). One published pipeline composites an AI actor over a real app screen recording ([n8n workflow](https://videobgremover.com/n8n/ugc-ad-generator)).

## 3. Constraints for a 10-a-day unattended pipeline

| | Limit | 10/day? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | ~15 Direct Posts per creator per day, shared across all apps; unaudited apps can only post SELF_ONLY | Fits, but posts stay private until audit | [TikTok guidelines](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-sharing-guidelines) |
| Instagram | 50 API posts per rolling 24h (some Meta pages say 100) | Fits | [Meta reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/content_publishing_limit) |
| YouTube | Default 100 `videos.insert` calls per day (separate bucket); unaudited uploads stay private | Fits, but private until audit | [YouTube quota costs](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost) |

**AI video availability and cost today.**
- OpenAI shut down the Sora app and API in September 2026 ([Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/sora-pricing), third-party).
- Google's pricing page now lists **Gemini Omni Flash** (`gemini-omni-1.1-flash`) at about **$0.10 per second of 720p video** ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Veo prices no longer appear there.
- Image models cost about $0.03–0.13 per image (same page).
- A fully AI 15-second ad with a few retakes is roughly $3–5, which at 10 ads a day is about $1,000–1,500 a month. Templates built on real screenshots cost close to nothing per render. *(Estimate.)*

**AI labels.**
- TikTok requires a label on realistic AI content. Vieews already sends `is_aigc`.
- YouTube asks uploaders to declare "altered or synthetic" content. Vieews doesn't send `containsSyntheticMedia` yet.
- Meta auto-labels photorealistic AI video as "AI info".
- Stylized motion graphics generally don't need a label.
- Sources: [Cinerads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ad-disclosure-requirements), [Metricool](https://metricool.com/how-to-label-ai-content/). These are secondary sources, so check each platform's help page.

## Key takeaways for the skill

1. **The concept comes before the prompt.** Every winning example started with an angle tied to a core desire, plus comedy or contrast. The skill has to generate concepts, not just render templates.
2. **Use a fixed structure and vary the content.** Hook in 0–3s, magic moment by 5–10s, then payoff. Variety comes from the angle, hook, format, persona, setting and tone.
3. **Self-contained shot prompts, 3–5 at a time,** each re-describing camera, place, character, action and one line of dialogue.
4. **Overgenerate and pick.** The pros kept 5–25% of takes. Unattended, Claude has to judge its own output by looking at frames before posting.
5. **Volume plus memory.** Winning teams post often and keep a log of what they tried. The skill needs a log of used combinations so it doesn't repeat itself.
6. **The cheap formats won for apps.** Slideshows and screen-led demos drove real installs. Cinematic AI video gets press, but costs ~100× more per ad.

## Round 2: formats that drove installs, and a free stack

**Founder-reported formats** (most come from [GTM Sauce](https://gtmsauce.com/topics/tiktok-slideshows)'s collection of founder interviews on the Superwall, App Masters and Starter Story podcasts; figures are as the founders stated them):
- **GlamUp.** One before/after slideshow with a fixed hook (`never saw myself with the / right makeup routine / 😭😭`), posted across about 20 accounts. The first post got about 10M views; the app reached 1M users in 6 months.
- **Stronger.** 725 posts in 295 days, 98% of the winners slideshows: a selfie with a short tension hook, then the app on slide 2–3. 11 posts passed 1M views ([Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/the-slideshow-strategy-that-generated-700000-users-in-275-days)).
- **Flowfy / Alberto Pérez.** 100K downloads from slideshows. Test 5–6 formats in two weeks and repeat what clears 15K views ([Tap & Swipe](https://tap-and-swipe.com/episodes/alberto-tiktok-slideshows)).
- **Knownwell.** iMessage-drama slideshows with an app resolution: 438K and 104K views ([PlayKit](https://playkit.beehiiv.com/p/ai-journal-app)).
- **Halo AI.** One fixed format (absurd AI image, in-app demo, text-story twist): 1.2B views in 120 days ([GTM Sauce hooks](https://gtmsauce.com/topics/video-hooks)).
- **SideShift.** A non-talking text hook ("I have a master's degree and couldn't find a job"); the talking version later got 9.6M views.
- **FaithFull.** Its top Meta ad was an Arcads AI actor; a human remake of the same script beat it.
- **Shot Pattern.** An unpolished screen-recording tutorial took 80–90% of ad spend at $1.57 per install ([GTM Sauce ad creative](https://gtmsauce.com/topics/ad-creative)).
- **Conversion caveat.** Demo videos converted 0.5–1% of viewers vs 0.05–0.2% for slideshows (Flamme); a demo converted about 20× better than slideshows (Lovely) ([GTM Sauce short-form](https://gtmsauce.com/topics/short-form-video)).
- **Larry (Oliver Henry).** An agent that made 6-slide slideshows: one shared scene description across image prompts, text overlays added afterwards, drafts plus a manual trending sound, and a views → downloads feedback loop. It reported 500K–8M views ([Stack Junkie](https://www.stack-junkie.com/blog/how-to-automate-tiktok-marketing-with-openclaw-larry-skill-deep-dive)).

**Legal.** The FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (16 CFR 465, in force Oct 21, 2024) bans testimonials that misrepresent the reviewer's existence or experience, including AI-generated ones ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/ftc-publishes-final-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)).

**Free stack, checked Oct 30, 2026:**
- Cloudflare Workers AI gives 10,000 free neurons a day. `flux-2-klein-4b` takes width/height and up to 4 reference images; `flux-1-schnell` makes squares only ([pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)).
- Kokoro-82M runs in Node on the CPU through `kokoro-js` (Apache-2.0).
- Claude Code cloud scheduled tasks run on 4 vCPU / 16 GB VMs with no GPU, at most hourly, in auto mode. They support network secrets and cached setup scripts ([docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)). Open-source *talking-video* models aren't practical there, so AI people appear as photos in the non-talking formats above.

## Methodology
Queries covered viral AI ads and their prompts (Kalshi/Veo, Sora/Veo UGC), app growth on TikTok (Cal AI, Umax, slideshows), AI UGC performance, platform publishing limits and AI-label rules. 24 sources were used and 4 were read in full. Performance figures from vendors and agencies are labeled as such. I found no independent, measured study of AI-generated app ads.
